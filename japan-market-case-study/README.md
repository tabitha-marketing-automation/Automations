# Japan Market Case Study: HubSpot

**What this is:** a desk-research case study on how HubSpot approaches Japan, who the likely buyer is, and three tests I would run. It uses public sources only (accessed 31 Oct 2026). I have no access to HubSpot's internal data, so anything I am guessing is marked **hypothesis**.

## 1. Product

HubSpot sells a CRM platform with marketing, sales and service tools. A free CRM is the entry point, and paid plans add automation and analytics. It is aimed at growing companies that want one system for customer data.

## 2. English site vs Japanese site

I compared hubspot.com and hubspot.jp (Japanese page read in translation).

| | English site | Japanese site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline | "Build demand, win deals, and delight customers, all on one platform" | The same message, translated closely |
| Main calls to action | Get a demo / Get started free | The same two |
| How free and pricing are shown | "Free and premium plans", no prices on the page | States that the free CRM supports up to 1,000 contacts |
| Customer proof | eBay, DoorDash, Reddit, Tripadvisor, Eventbrite. Case studies: Unipart, Angel City FC, Youth on Course | HIS, Sansan, Money Forward, Meiko Gijuku, Eisai, NTT Data. Case studies: Panasonic Industry, PCA |
| Trust signals | 306,000+ customers, "#1 in 526 G2 reports" | The same global numbers, plus 135 countries and 2,000+ integrations. No Japan-specific customer count or rating |
| Contact and support | Contact sales form, demo. No phone or live chat on the page | The same, plus a Japanese help center, community, free courses, Japanese webinars and a partner search |

**What I take from it:** the proof and support are localised (Japanese customers, Japanese help, partners), but the headline and the numbers are global. **Hypothesis:** the numbers do not tell a Japanese buyer how many companies like theirs use it.

## 3. Competitors in Japan

From a Japanese comparison article by [AIsmiley](https://aismiley.co.jp/ai_news/ma-tool-comparison-table/):

| Tool | How it positions itself |
| --- | --- |
| SATORI | Reaches anonymous website visitors, not only people who gave their name |
| SHANON Marketing Platform | A Japan-based platform claiming 700+ client companies and a top share of integrated marketing support SaaS |
| Pardot (Salesforce) | Follows up prospects through tracking of their behaviour and sales campaigns |
| Adobe Marketing Cloud | Manages content for many channels in one place |

Pricing differs a lot. The article lists some tools in the tens of thousands of yen per month and others in the hundreds of thousands, with several priced only on request.

## 4. Likely buyer (hypothesis)

- **Who:** marketing or IT/digital teams at companies with 50+ employees. The 2026 survey below only covered companies of that size, and a [failure analysis](https://it-trend.jp/marketing_automation_tool/article/marketing-automation-failures) names marketing and IT departments as typical users.
- **Worry 1, hidden cost:** buyers rank clear prices, extra fees and contract terms as the most trusted information (60.6%).
- **Worry 2, nobody to run it:** the same analysis says tools are abandoned when setup takes too long or no one owns content and scoring.
- **Worry 3, proving it works:** vendor content is expected to give price guidance (42.9%), and 30.2% want an ROI explanation from sales.

## 5. How buyers are reached

Evidence from [HubSpot Japan's 2026 B2B purchasing survey](https://ecnomikata.com/ecnews/51145/) (1,573 people at companies of 50+ employees, run by Macromill):

1. **Generative AI and AI search.** The most-cited new research behaviour (33.5%). ChatGPT is used often or sometimes by 58.0%, Gemini by 51.2%.
2. **Transparent vendor content.** Clear prices and terms (60.6%) and vendor case studies (57.0%) are the most trusted.
3. **Sales conversations and partners.** Individual explanations from vendor sales staff are trusted by 59.8%. HubSpot runs a Solutions Partner programme in Japan.

The survey has no numbers for webinars or trade shows, so I have not claimed any.

## 6. Three tests

1. **If** we add a Japanese pricing section that shows total cost in yen, including extra fees and contract terms, **then** more visitors request a demo, **measured by** pricing-page-to-demo conversion.
2. **If** we publish case studies from Japanese mid-size companies with ROI figures and a short "who this is not for" note, **then** more readers become qualified leads, **measured by** the SQL rate of case-study readers.
3. **If** we add Japanese comparison and FAQ pages written for AI search, **then** AI-referred visits convert to demos, **measured by** sessions from AI tools and their demo conversion.

## Limits

Desk research only. The Japanese site was read in translation and I did not verify claims such as customer counts. Prices from third-party articles can be out of date. All three tests are untested ideas.
